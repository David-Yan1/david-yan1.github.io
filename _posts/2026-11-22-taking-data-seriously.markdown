Is data finally ascendant? Large-scale data has proven to be an effective engine of progress in language modeling, image generation, representation learning, and more. Data vendors, middlemen, and flippers are making billions of dollars selling every kind of data under the sun to frontier labs. And on social media, esteemed researchers often espouse the importance of "looking at the data." So it seems we have all come to appreciate the value of data!

However, many people are still not taking data seriously. Although it is commonly understood that data is important, the vast majority of researchers still work on improving the learner while treating the data as fixed. Ironically, people are more vigilant than ever before about using filtered, high-quality training data, but only insofar as it enables better study of their architectures and algorithms! This is especially true in open-source research spaces like academia, which leaves most insights from data research locked behind the closed doors of industry labs.

Data should not be mere means to the end of studying algorithms. Rather, we ought to **study data as we study architectures and learning algorithms**. At a practical level, this means orienting research projects around the end goal of (1) understanding properties of data and (2) using these insights to produce more effective data recipes. How do we construct data that yields significant improvements in learning efficiency? Can we move inductive bias into data and encourage networks to learn desirable solutions? Just as the careful study of architectures led to new insights and advances in architecture design, I believe that data research will yield breakthroughs in dataset design.

To realize this goal, researchers should make **ambitious data bets**, similar to the ambitious architecture or algorithm bets of the past. One data bet that I am excited about is procedural data, which is generated by symbolic program. Procedural data generation is a scalable method of converting compute into data and is fully controllable, making it easy to study. Recent advances in coding agents have also significantly lowered the cost and effort required to write expressive and interesting procedural programs. Of course, this is just one of many possible approaches. Reaching a scientific understanding of data will require both empirical and theoretical advances, and will undoubtedly require many more people and many more ideas. So I'll end on a simple ask: we should all take data seriously!
